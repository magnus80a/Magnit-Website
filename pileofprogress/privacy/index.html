Pile of Progress Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 27, 2026

Pile of Progress is made by MagnIT AB, a Swedish company. Everything you add to Pile of Progress stays on your device, unless your own device backup copies it. We have no servers and no accounts, so your collections, notes and photos never reach us. The app has no ads, no tracking and no analytics of ours, it sends us nothing, and we don’t sell data. The App Store or Google Play handles the purchase.

What Pile of Progress stores on your device

  • Your pile: the names of your collections, armies and units, and how many miniatures of each unit are at each stage.
  • Your workflow: the names and order of your stages.
  • Notes: the text you write on a collection, army or unit, such as a paint scheme.
  • Photos: the one photo you can give each unit.
  • The purchase: that the unlock has been bought, so the app stays unlocked without a connection. On Android this is kept in the app’s storage, with the date if Google Play no longer lists the purchase. On iPhone it’s kept in the phone’s keychain.

All of this is kept on your device, where other apps can’t read it. We can’t see it, and Pile of Progress doesn’t send it anywhere. The app works without an internet connection, except for buying or restoring the unlock.

Camera and photos

Pile of Progress asks for camera access the first time you take a photo of a unit. Without it, the app still works, but you can’t take photos in it. You can withdraw it at any time in your device’s settings. Pile of Progress doesn’t use the microphone.

When you pick a photo from your library instead, your device’s own photo picker hands over only the photo you choose, not the rest of your library. Pile of Progress saves a square, smaller copy of it in its own storage and leaves the original in your library as it is.

The purchase

Pile of Progress is free to download and holds up to 10 model types, which this policy calls units. One purchase, Unlock the whole pile, removes that limit. It is sold through the App Store or Google Play, under Apple’s or Google’s privacy policy. The app never sees your name, email address or payment details.

The app asks the store for the price, to buy or restore the unlock, and whether you have bought it. It asks whether you have bought it each time you open the app or come back to it. If your pile is unlimited because you used the app before it had the limit, the app doesn’t ask the store anything. On iPhone it does this through Apple’s StoreKit, which is part of iOS. On Android it does it through Google’s Play Billing Library, which is part of the app. The app reaches both through expo-iap, an open-source library that runs on your device and, as we use it, sends nothing of its own.

On Android, Google’s Play Billing Library also sends Google a technical log over an encrypted connection: how its requests to Google Play went, the app and library versions, and details of the device such as its model, memory, Android version, language, time zone, network type and mobile operator. It can’t be turned off in the app. It contains nothing you add to Pile of Progress, and we don’t receive it.

Device backups

What you add to Pile of Progress, including your photos, can be part of your device’s own backup: iCloud on iPhone, and Google’s backup on Android. If you’ve turned these backups on, copies are stored in your Apple or Google account under Apple’s or Google’s privacy policy. We have no access to them. You can turn these backups off in your device’s settings. What the app keeps about the purchase isn’t in Android’s backup. An iPhone’s backup can hold it, but it only comes back on that same iPhone. Android backs up at most 25 MB per app; above that, none of Pile of Progress’s data is backed up.

App stores

Apple and Google handle your download of Pile of Progress under their own privacy policies. They give us statistics about installs, and may give us crash reports from people who’ve agreed to share them. They also give us reports of what was sold: the product, the price, the date, and the country. From Google Play these are per order, with an order number, the device model and in some cases the state, city and postcode. None of this includes your name, your payment details, or your collections, notes or photos. We use the sales reports to keep our accounts and to handle questions and refunds.

Who we share data with

Nobody. We don’t receive what you add, so we have nothing of it to share or sell. Apple and Google handle data as described above, in their own services, and Google’s billing code on Android sends Google the log described above.

Data retention and deletion

Your data stays on your device until you delete it. In the app, you can remove a unit’s photo, or delete a unit, an army or a collection with everything in it, photos included. Deleting the app removes everything you added from your device. We can’t delete it for you or get it back, because we don’t have it.

On iPhone, what the app keeps about the purchase may remain in the keychain after you delete the app. It says only that the unlock has been bought, with nothing about you or your pile. If you install the app again on that iPhone, it may open unlocked, also with another Apple Account.

Copies in your iCloud or Google backup are kept and deleted according to those services, and you can delete them there.

Your rights

You control your data in Pile of Progress directly, in the app. The only records we have are the stores’ reports described above, which don’t include your name. You’re welcome to email us with questions, or to ask what we hold about an order if you send us its number. If you’re unhappy with how we handle your data, you can complain to the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (IMY) at imy.se.

Security

Pile of Progress’s data is kept in app storage, and on iPhone in the keychain, where other apps can’t read it, and your device’s lock protects it. The app doesn’t add a lock or encryption of its own, so anyone who can unlock your device can open it.

Children

Pile of Progress is a general hobby app. We collect no data through it from anyone, children included.

Changes

When this policy changes, we update this page and the date at the top.
